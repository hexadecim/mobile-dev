5 KPIs Every Software Development Company Should Track for Project Profitability

By Aganya Solutions · Updated August 14, 2026 · 8 min read

Most software development and IT services companies already track utilization and revenue. Fewer connect the two into a single, project-level profitability number — and that's the number that actually predicts whether the business grows or stalls. Below are the five KPIs delivery leads and finance teams should be watching every week, not just at quarter close.

1. Project margin (revenue minus fully-loaded cost)

Project margin is revenue recognized on a project minus its fully-loaded cost — salaries, benefits, overhead, contractor rates, and any cloud or tooling spend directly attributable to the work. Tracking this at the project level, not just at the company level, is what surfaces the individual engagements quietly dragging down an otherwise healthy portfolio.

A company running at a healthy 25% blended margin can still have three or four projects bleeding money underneath that average.

2. Effective billing rate vs. cost rate

Compare what you're actually billing per hour (after discounts, scope changes, and non-billable rework) against your fully-loaded cost per hour for the people doing the work. The gap between these two numbers, tracked by role and by project, tells you whether your rate card still reflects reality — especially as seniority mix shifts over a project's lifecycle.

3. Budget burn rate vs. percent complete

For fixed-bid work, the single earliest warning sign of a losing project is budget burn outpacing delivery progress. If a project has consumed 70% of its budget at 45% completion, that's not a rounding error — it's a trend that needs a scope conversation before it becomes a write-off.

4. Bench and non-billable time by account

Bench time between engagements, PTO, and internal tooling work often get lumped into general overhead — which hides which clients or project types are actually causing it. Attributing non-billable time back to the account or project that created it (a slow ramp-up, frequent scope pauses) gives you a much more honest margin picture.

5. Client lifetime margin, not just client revenue

A client that generates significant revenue isn't automatically your most valuable client — not if servicing them requires senior resources at a discount, frequent scope renegotiation, or a support burden that eats margin elsewhere. Ranking clients by cumulative margin, not top-line revenue, usually reorders the "who matters most" list inside most services businesses.

Putting it together

Individually, each of these metrics is useful. Together — updated in real time and viewable by project, client, and team — they turn profitability from a quarterly surprise into something a delivery lead can manage week to week.
